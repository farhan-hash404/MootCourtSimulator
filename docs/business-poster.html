The Problem

Law graduates qualify without ever having argued.

Advocacy is a performance skill, but Pakistani legal education assesses it on paper. A student can pass every exam and still have never faced an objection.

No repeatable practice

Moot courts need a judge, opposing counsel and witnesses in one room. Most students get a handful of moots in five years — some get none.

The bar filter is brutal

LAW-GAT — mandatory for bar enrolment — has seen pass rates widely reported near 20–30%. The gap is applied advocacy, not memorised doctrine.

Generic AI misquotes the law

Students already use ChatGPT. It invents Pakistani sections confidently. A tool that misquotes a statute to a law student is worse than no tool.

The Solution

One human. The court supplies everyone else.

A moot court has always needed five people free at the same hour, in the same room. That single logistical fact is why students get so little practice. We remove four of those five — the student is the only human the hearing requires.

What removing four humans actually buys

No scheduling

Nobody has to be free. A student can hold a full hearing at two in the morning, the night before an exam.

Unlimited repetition

The same case can be argued ten different ways. Advocacy is a skill built by repetition, which the old model cannot supply.

One consistent bench

Every student faces the same standard. Marking stops depending on which faculty member happened to be free.

Near-zero marginal cost

The fifth hearing costs the same as the first — under three rupees a turn. Four salaries do not scale; this does.

“Unlimited” with a 12-hour cooldown — what it costs us at the extreme

Typical · 6 hearings

Costs PKR 480 against PKR 5,600. 91% margin. This is what the revenue model assumes.

Heavy · 20 hearings

Costs PKR 1,600. 71% margin. A student in exam week, practising most days.

Ceiling · 60 hearings

Every cooldown used, all month. Costs PKR 4,800. Still profitable at 14%.

This is why the cooldown exists. Truly unlimited access makes the cost of a subscriber unbounded — one determined user could cost more than they pay. A hearing every 12 hours reads as unlimited to a student who will never use two a day, and caps our worst possible customer below the subscription price. Why the margin holds as we grow

Cost scales per turn, not per user — an idle account costs nothing, and a free account costs pennies. The objection cascade already routes routine screening to a cheaper model, cutting ~9% off spend with no measured quality loss.

The honest risk

$20 is premium pricing for this market, so the number to watch is conversion off the free tier, not margin. The model needs roughly 3 in 100 Basic users to upgrade. The pilot exists to test exactly that — and Enterprise is the hedge if it comes in low.

Defensible corpus

53 provisions of QSO, PPC, CrPC and the Constitution diffed word-for-word against official prints. That verification is the moat — it is slow, manual work a competitor must repeat.

Adversarial by architecture

A LangGraph multi-agent graph, not one model wearing hats. Objection → ruling → testimony is a routed path, which no single chatbot completion can reproduce.

Priced for this market

Built to run at under one cent per turn, so it can be sold in rupees to Pakistani institutions and still hold margin.
